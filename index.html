? Help · how to use

1. Pick a thread. Tap the size and start typing — 1/4-20, m6, 1/2 npt or a diameter like .375 all work. Your recent picks and the most-used sizes are pinned at the top; every UNC, UNF, UNEF, ISO metric, NPT, BSPT and BSPP size is below, grouped by series.

2. Cut or form tap. A cut tap cuts the thread out. A form tap pushes the metal into shape (no chips), so it needs a bigger drill. Default % thread: 75% cut, 65% form. You can type 50–100%.

3. Read the drill. You get the exact drill size, the nearest standard drills just under and just over (fraction / number / letter and metric), and the real % thread each one gives.

  • Green = good. Amber = check it (shallow thread, or a pipe thread that uses a table drill). Red = high torque, the tap may snap.
  • More than ~75% thread barely adds strength but adds a lot of tap torque.
  • Pipe threads (NPT, BSPT, BSPP) don't use the % formula. The app shows the published table drill. Tapered threads: check depth with an L1 plug gauge.

4. Speeds & feeds. Set the work material and its hardness once (HB or HRC). The tap panel gives RPM and the rigid-tap feed (feed = RPM × pitch, exactly) and a Fanuc G84 block. The drill panel gives SFM, RPM, IPR, IPM and a peck plan.

Every speed and feed is a conservative starting point from published maker charts (Morse, Redline, Tru-Edge, Haas, OSG, Gühring, Emuge). Harder material lowers the numbers or blocks them. Always check your own tool maker's chart and listen to the cut.

in | mm in the header flips every length, feed and the G-code (G20 / G21).
